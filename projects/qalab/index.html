Begin with the real question

A useful standard starts by asking how much evidence this particular change needs.

Risk-based test design · production practice

Can we expect two independent QA engineers to complete the same task with the same result?

Imagine two identical parallel universes with one exception: the QA engineer assigned to the task. How can we shape the QA process for an AAA system with a complex Core Gameplay structure so that we can expect the same result in both universes?

My experience in AAA suggests that the individual specialist has too much influence on the outcome. So what can we do about it?

The original problem

A universal AAA checklist does not exist.

A check is not inherently mandatory or optional.

This makes it important not to settle for the apparently safest conclusion: test everything. In an AAA environment, deciding what does not need to be tested is the hardes – and perhaps the most importan – question.

This framework builds that decision around three parameters:

Depth defines vertical coverage.

It determines how many rules, states, boundaries, combinations and failure paths must be investigated inside the changed functionality.

Distrust is the space for individuality and QA expertise. It is always applied on top of the depth and breadth calculation – and it remains visible, explainable and open to discussion.

Where did the idea come from?The initial question grew from my work at Strikerz on UFL, specifically within Core Gameplay QA. Independent public workThe framework was substantially redesigned and discloses no internal data, processes or implementation details.

Interactive model

Estimate the minimum test scope

Choose what the change can affect and how difficult it is to implement safely.

01
Impact measures consequence.

Player exposure, affected systems, persistence, business risk and the cost of an unnoticed failure. It answers how far regression can travel.

02
Complexity measures uncertainty.

Rules, states, branches, dependencies, timing and technical novelty. It answers how many ways the implementation can behave unexpectedly.

Why 60 / 40? Impact is weighted higher because testing scope should primarily protect the product from consequences. Complexity increases uncertainty, but difficult code with a tiny blast radius should not automatically receive release-wide regression.

Calibration, not dogma

The values may be calibrated.
The reasoning must remain shared.

01Start with 60 / 40

Impact is weighted higher because consequence and regression breadth take priority over implementation difficulty.

02Validate historically

Compare recommendations with escaped defects, over-testing costs and real tasks from the product.

03Change as a team

Weights may evolve, but they cannot vary invisibly from one engineer or task to another.

The framework standardizes the reasoning – not the person.

It establishes a minimum guaranteed level while keeping expert distrust visible and discussable.

Functional tests vs. Behavioral tests

Functional checks may produce the expected result, but that does not guarantee that behavioral checks will do the same. In other words, testing a feature canno – and should no – end with a binary true/false or passed/failed result. What happens afterward is just as important.
